When the heat goes out in Somerville, MA, the clock starts running, and the diagnosis depends entirely on what kind of system is doing the failing. On a gas furnace, the single most frequent culprit is a flame sensor coated in oxide: the burners light, the sensor cannot prove the flame, and the board shuts the gas down after the third attempt and locks out. Close behind come cracked hot surface igniters, stuck pressure switches, clogged condensate traps on high-efficiency models, and blower motors that have quietly worn out. Heat pumps fail differently: a weak defrost cycle leaves the outdoor coil buried under frost, a failed reversing valve strands the system in cooling, and a dead outdoor unit leaves the electric backup strips carrying the whole house at several times the operating cost.

Signs You Need Heating Repair in Somerville

Spotting the early warning signs lets you act before the problem gets expensive. Watch for these telltale signs:

  • Cold rooms in one part of the house while others are comfortable
  • Heating bills spiking while comfort keeps falling
  • Banging at startup caused by burners lighting late
  • An outdoor heat pump coil that stays iced over
  • No warm air at the registers even with the heat turned up
  • Strange smells from the vents, especially rotten egg or burning
  • A furnace that tries to light, clicks, and then quits
  • Short cycling every few minutes while the house stays cold

The Benefits of Professional Heating Repair

Heating repair carries the highest stakes in home comfort work because combustion is involved. A furnace that lights late booms gas vapor inside the heat exchanger and stresses the metal; a heat exchanger that cracks can pass combustion gases into the supply air; a flue that has stopped drafting sends carbon monoxide into the house instead of out of it, and none of these announce themselves in ways an untrained eye can catch. The diagnostic terrain is genuinely tricky too: a furnace that short-cycles might have a failing limit switch, or it might be protecting itself from a clogged filter, and cleaning a flame sensor without asking why it fouled leaves the next lockout already scheduled.

That is why Somerville HVAC does not guess. Our technicians read flame signal on a meter, look at the heat exchanger directly, confirm the flue is drafting, and compare temperature rise to the data plate to make sure the blower speed fits the burner. On heat pumps we check defrost controls, sensors, and the reversing valve before blaming the compressor, and we find out whether the electric backup heat has been running in the background and driving up your costs. Nothing is called fixed until it has run a full cycle and proven itself safe and correct, because your family's warmth and safety depend on it.

Homeowner Tips

Maintenance Tips for Heating Repair

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Give the outdoor heat pump unit open space, free of snow drifts and plantings, so it can defrost
  • Have the system serviced each autumn, before the weather tests every weak component
  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly and put one on every level of the home
  • Move solvents, paints, and pool or laundry chemicals away from the furnace to protect the burners from corrosive vapor
  • Change the filter monthly in heating season; starved airflow trips limit switches and cracks exchangers early
  • Call about booming at startup or new rattles right away, while the fix is still inexpensive
  • Keep the area around the furnace clear so the burner gets adequate combustion air
